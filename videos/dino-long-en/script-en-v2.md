## 0 · The Breathing

Day ten.
The ground shakes. Dirt pours from the ceiling of your tunnel.
Outside, the sky is on fire.
And at the door, something too big to fit inside is breathing.
(pause)
Ten days ago, you were asleep in your own bed.

You have 10 days before the asteroid that killed the dinosaurs. Every big animal you see will die.
You won't. If you stay small.

## 1 · Ten Days

The night before was like any other. Homework, a documentary about the dinosaurs, your phone at 23%. "I'll charge it in the morning."
In your backpack, ready for school: two textbooks, a sandwich, two chocolate bars, half a bottle of water.
And a library book you wanted to read in the break tomorrow. *The Last Day of the Dinosaurs.*

[DAY 1/10]
In the morning, there's no ceiling. There are leaves, each one bigger than your bed.
The air is hot and wet. No cars. No voices.
Next to you, something rises from the ground like a tree trunk.
It isn't a tree. It has claws.
The foot lifts, slams down one step ahead, and the ground jumps under you.
You don't breathe until the footsteps fade.

Your phone is still in your pocket. No signal. No internet.
Just one message that wasn't there last night.
"Impact: 10 days. 3,000 kilometers south."
[🔋 23%]
A prank. A dream. It has to be.
You switch it off at 22%, and you start walking.

All afternoon, you look for a road, a wire, a plane in the sky. Anything.
There's nothing.
In the evening, you find the owner of the foot. A T. rex. 12 meters, nose to tail, with a long pale scar across its snout.
For one second, its head turns toward you.
Then it walks on.
It's not a dream.

That night you squeeze in between the roots of a giant tree. You're shaking. You don't want to sleep.
In the dark, you open your backpack, looking for anything that makes sense.
The library book.
On the cover, a T. rex skeleton in a museum. You don't look closely.
Inside, chapter seven. *Who Survived.*
Animals that were small. Animals that hid underground. Animals that lived near fresh water.
On the back page, with your school pen, you write a plan.
[LIST · DAY 1: SHELTER, 1 m of soil overhead ☐ · WATER ☐ · FOOD for the dark ☐]
Three things. Ten days. None of them done.
You don't decide to fall asleep. Your body decides for you.

## 2 · The Smallest Thing in the Forest

[DAY 2/10]
In the morning, there's a small hole by the roots.
A furry face pokes out. Mouse-sized. Sniffs. Disappears.
You name him Pip.

The T. rex needs mountains of meat every day.
Pip needs a few seeds and a hole to hide in.
And your book agrees. Scientists think that after the impact, almost no land animal heavier than about 25 kilograms survived.
You weigh 70.
So you'll have to think like Pip.
Rule one: stay small.

You eat half the sandwich. You save the rest.
Then you watch what Pip eats. Small red berries, seeds from one kind of fern.
If they don't hurt him, they're probably safe for you. You fill your pockets.

[DAY 3/10]
Day three. The bottle is empty. Your throat is burning.
You follow the sound of water to a wide, muddy river.
At the edge, a log is floating.
It blinks.
It isn't a log. It's a crocodile.

So you don't drink from the river.
A few steps back from the bank, in the sand, you dig a pit and wait.
Water seeps in through the sand. Slow. Muddy. But cleaner than the river, and far from the teeth.
In the shallow pools, small fish are trapped. You catch three and dry them on a hot rock. No fire needed. It's warm here, almost tropical, even at night.
[LIST: WATER ☑ · FOOD 3 fish]
The book says freshwater animals, turtles, frogs, even crocodiles, made it through far better than most.
Rule two: stay near water.

## 3 · A Shovel Made of Dinosaur

[DAY 4/10]
Day four. The ground shakes again. Not one footstep. Hundreds.
A herd of duck-billed dinosaurs comes along the river. Then a deep sound rolls in from far away. You feel it in your chest more than your ears.
The herd panics. Trees snap. You throw yourself behind a fallen log.
When the dust settles, one of them didn't get up.
And it has something you need. A shoulder blade. Flat. Wide. The size of a paddle.
From today, your shovel is a dinosaur.

[DAY 5/10]
Day five. You find a clay bank above the river and start digging.
Then you look at the trees.
Every trunk has a line of dried mud on it. Above your head.
The river floods. Right up to here.
You lose the afternoon moving higher.

Now the math.
With a bone, in clay, you dig about 30 centimeters a day.
A tunnel deep enough to put a meter of soil over your head is about two and a half meters.
That's eight days of work.
The rock hits on day ten. You have five.
[LIST: SHELTER 5% · BEHIND]

You dig until it's too dark to see, then sleep in the mud by the hole.

[DAY 6/10]
Day six. Your hands are blistered. Half a meter in.
Then the roof comes down on you.
You crawl out coughing dirt.
A whole day of work, gone.
Four days left. Seven days of digging.

You sit in the mud and look at Pip's hole. It's tiny. It never collapses.
Because Pip doesn't dig a room. Pip digs exactly what Pip needs.
So you stop digging a room.
You dig a tunnel exactly as wide as your shoulders. Half the dirt. Twice as fast.
And you shape the roof into an arch, because an arch pushes the weight into the walls instead of down on you.
The entrance faces north, away from where the rock will hit.
Stay small. Even when you dig.
[LIST: SHELTER 40% · BACK ON TRACK]

That night, for the first time, you sleep inside.
It's tight. It smells of wet clay. The river is a whisper through the walls.
And for the first time in six days, nothing can step on you.
Rule three: stay underground.

## 4 · Eleven Percent

[DAY 7/10]
Day seven. Pip doesn't run anymore. He's waiting at the entrance in the morning.
You split your last chocolate with him.
You tell him about home. Your room. Your school. The phone you forgot to charge.
Then you switch it on and show him photos. Your room. A street. People.
He doesn't answer. But he listens.
When you switch it off, it's at 11%.
[🔋 11%]
You spent ten percent on photos of home.
It was worth it.

That night, you wake up and he's asleep in your jacket pocket. Warm. Breathing.
Seven days in a world with no one. And now you have someone.

## 5 · The Thing in the Tunnel

[DAY 8/10]
Day eight. Midnight. You wake at the far end of the tunnel.
Scratching at the entrance. Then sniffing.
Then something coming in.
In the dark, two yellow eyes.
A feathered hunter. Fast. Only as tall as your knee.
Not a T. rex. Small enough to fit in your tunnel.
Small doesn't mean safe.

It lunges. Claws rip across your right forearm.
No knife. No fire.
Just one thing this world has never seen.
You switch the phone on. 11%.
You hit the alarm and point the flashlight straight into its face.
A sound sixty-six million years too early fills the tunnel.
It shrieks, and it's gone.

But you don't dare switch the light off.
Blood drips from your arm. Pip creeps out of your pocket and sits by your hand.
The light is eating the battery. At this rate, it dies before dawn.
You hold it on the entrance until the sky turns grey.
[🔋 3%]
3%.

[DAY 9/10]
Day nine. You have a fever. The wound is hot. You wrap it in a strip of your sleeve.
The entrance let a hunter in. Tomorrow, it has to keep the whole world out.
One-handed, you drag stones up from the river and stack them around the opening.
Last, a big flat stone. From inside, you can drag it across like a door, and push it aside to get out.
When it's closed, it leaves a gap a hand wide. Enough for air. Not enough for teeth.
The sun is going down when the door slides into place.
[LIST: SHELTER 100% ☑ · WATER ☑ · FOOD 4 fish]
Just in time.

## 6 · The Last Normal Morning

You switch the phone on one more time. The message has changed.
"Impact: tomorrow."
[🔋 2%]
You switch it off.

That night you slide the door aside and climb out. The sky is full of stars. From across the river comes that low, familiar call. The same T. rex.
The last night of the dinosaurs.
And only you know.

[DAY 10/10]
It isn't a strange morning. That's what makes it strange.
Birds are singing. Insects are buzzing. A few triceratops are drinking across the river.
None of them know.

Then, in the south, a light.
Not like the sun. Like someone switched the sky on by mistake.
[LIST: IMPACT +0 MIN]

Your book said the shaking comes within minutes. It's right.
[+6 MIN]
The ground starts to move. Not like a footstep. Like the whole world.
The river pulls back. Then it comes back, the wrong way. A wall of water runs upstream and throws fish onto the bank.
It climbs to the mud line on the trees, and stops a hand below your door.
When it pulls back, you run down and grab every fish you can carry. Food for the dark.
[LIST: FOOD 7 fish]
This isn't imagination. In North Dakota, scientists found fossil fish with tiny glass beads from this impact stuck in their gills.

[+20 MIN]
Then those same glass beads start falling from the sky. Small and hot, like burning hail.
The sky turns red.
You drag the door shut, hold Pip tight and crawl to the end of the tunnel.
[+45 MIN]
Outside, the first trees catch fire.

And then, outside, footsteps. Heavy. Slow. Close.
Something huge collapses at your door, and its shadow covers the gap.
The T. rex. From day one. The scar across its snout.
Its huge snout is right against the stones. Its hot breath fills your tunnel.
It can't get in. It doesn't even try.
It only came for the cool soil at your door, away from the fire.

All night, from the other side of the stones, you hear it breathing.
Each breath slower.
(pause)
Until morning.
(pause)
Then it stops.
The last dinosaur you saw died at your door.

## 7 · Stay Small

In the days after, the river rises again and buries the T. rex in thick mud.
You open your book to the cover. The museum skeleton.
And this time you look closely.
A long scar, across the snout.
(pause)
Sixty-six million years from now, children will stand in front of it in a museum.
One of them was you.

Then the dark comes.
Soot from fires all over the world, and dust from the impact, cover the whole sky. Sunlight can't get through. Plants can't grow. And it gets cold.
Scientists think this impact winter lasted months. Maybe years.
The world gave you one gift on its last day. Fire.
You carried an ember in from the burning forest. You keep a small fire right at the gap, never deep inside. In a closed tunnel, smoke kills quietly.
And at night, Pip sleeps against your neck. Two small bodies, keeping each other warm.

The phone won't turn on anymore.
So you scratch lines on the tunnel wall with your school pen.
Inventory.
Days after the end: 40. Still dark.
Dried fish: 7.
Dinosaurs left: zero.
Friends: one. Mouse-sized.

You repeat the rules to yourself. Stay small. Stay near water. Stay underground.
And a new one, that no book ever taught you.
Stay small… but don't stay alone.

Small animals like Pip made it out of this darkness. Millions of years later, from small mammals like him, came horses, whales, apes.
And people.
Sixty-six million years later, one of Pip's great-great-great-grandchildren is watching this video.

And just then, from outside the tunnel, a sound.
A bird, singing.
You cross out a line on the wall, and write it again.
Dinosaurs left: zero… except the birds.

On day one, what would you have done first? Tell me in the comments.
And if you want to know what you and Pip did after the dark, tell me that too.

Stay small. Stay alive.
