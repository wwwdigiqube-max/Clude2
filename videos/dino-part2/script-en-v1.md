## 0 · Seven Fish

[DAY 47]
The last fish is gone.
You scrape something white off the wall of your tunnel and hold it to the fire.
It's soft. It smells like the forest after rain.
Pip is watching you.
Seven days ago, you had seven fish.
(pause)
You have no food left. And nobody is coming.
At least, not for 66 million years.

## 1 · The Last of Them

[DAY 40]
Day forty. Seven fish on the shelf you cut into the clay. One a day. You did the math.
Outside, the sky is the colour of wet ash. Some mornings, the ash is snow.

You remember the first weeks after the impact.
You weren't the only one who made it through the fire.
A few dinosaurs did too. You saw them.
A duck-bill, so thin you could count its ribs, chewing on burnt branches.
A young horned dinosaur, standing in the river, just standing there.
But with no sun, nothing green grows. And without plants, there's nothing to eat.
The plant-eaters starved first. Then the hunters that ate them.
The bigger you were, the more food you needed, and the faster you died.
By day forty, you hadn't seen one in weeks.
That's what really killed the dinosaurs: not the fire, the hunger after it.

And it's the same hunger now, waiting at your door.

You used to lie in bed at night and imagine this. Dinosaurs, alive. Just once, just to see one.
You got your wish.
You saw them all.
And you watched every one of them die.

## 2 · Meanwhile

[NOW]
Sixty-six million years from now, your bedroom looks exactly the way you left it.
The posters. The unmade bed. The charger, still plugged into the wall.
Your mom hasn't moved anything.
There's a poster on every lamp post in town. Your face. "MISSING."
The police say you probably ran away.
Your mom says you'd never leave without your charger.

Your two best friends go back to the museum.
They stand in front of the T. rex, where you filmed that video.
"I'd survive. Easy."
Nobody laughs this time.

## 3 · Eat What Eats the Dead

[DAY 47]
Back to the tunnel. The last fish is gone.
One day with nothing. Then two.
Your hands start to shake. Pip gets thinner too.

Then, on day forty-nine, Pip does something strange.
He walks past the dead plants and goes straight for a rotten log.
He digs. And he comes out with a fat white grub.
Then another.
The forest is dead. But dead things are food, for something.
Beetles eat the rotting wood. Fungus eats the dead leaves. And the things that eat the dead are doing fine.
Scientists think that's exactly what happened. In the rivers and the soil, the animals that lived on dead things kept going, while everything that needed fresh plants starved.

So you dig next to Pip. Grubs. Beetles. And on the tunnel walls, that soft white fungus.
You don't know which fungus is safe. But Pip does. He eats the white kind. He won't touch the orange.
Neither will you.
Rule four: eat what eats the dead.
[LIST: FOOD: grubs, fungus · FISH: 0]

## 4 · A Message for Mom

[DAY 52]
With food in your stomach, you can think again.
And the thought that comes back, every night, is your mom.
She doesn't know where you are. She never will.
You can't go back. You've known it for weeks. Tonight, you say it out loud.
"I can't go back."
Pip looks up at you.

But maybe something can.
Your book said it: buried fast in mud, that's how bones become fossils.
And you have a bone. The one you've been digging with since day four.
So you take a sharp stone, and you start to write on your shovel.
It takes three nights. Your fingers bleed. You go over every letter again and again, deep enough to last forever.
Two words.
The same two words you said in front of the skeleton, laughing.
I SURVIVED.

## 5 · The River at Night

Now it has to be buried. Fast. In mud. Where the river floods.
That means going down to the water, in the dark.
The river is still running, grey and cold, with ice along the edges in the mornings.
And the river has survivors of its own.
The crocodile. And something worse. A long, thin snout, like a crocodile's, but narrower. A champsosaur. A fish hunter. It made it through too.
You wait until the moon is behind the smoke.
You crawl down the bank with the bone on your back.
You dig into the mud with your hands, right at the water's edge, where the river floods every spring.
Something moves in the water.
Pip squeaks. Once. Loud.
You throw yourself backwards, and the jaws snap shut where your hand was.
(pause)
The bone is in the mud. You push the last handful over it and run.

Back in the tunnel, you sit with your back to the stone door, shaking.
It's done. Whatever happens to you now, something of you will last.

## 6 · Not Alone

[DAY 60]
A few days later, Pip doesn't come back at night.
You wait. One night. Two.
On the third morning, he's at the entrance.
And he's not alone.
A second little face. Smaller. Same brown fur. Same black eyes.
She survived the dark too, in some other hole, under some other tree.
Pip isn't the last of his kind.
(pause)
You are happy for him. You really are.
But that night, the tunnel feels bigger than it ever has.

The cold is getting worse. The ice on the river doesn't melt by noon anymore.
Your tunnel was built for the fire. It wasn't built for winter.
You'll need somewhere deeper. Warmer.
But that's a problem for another day.
Tonight, you scratch a new line on the wall.
Friends: one.
You stop. And you add:
…and one more.

## 7 · Sixty-Six Million Years Later

[NOW]
North Dakota. A dig site, on a hot summer afternoon.
A paleontologist brushes the dust off the shoulder blade of a duck-billed dinosaur.
She stops.
There are scratches on the bone. Not teeth marks. Too straight. Too deep.
She calls the others over.
(pause)
If you could leave one message for 66 million years… what would you write on the bone?
Tell me in the comments.

Stay small. Stay alive.
