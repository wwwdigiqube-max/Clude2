# Season 1 · Part 2: The Asteroid Hit 40 Days Ago. You Have 7 Days of Food. (script EN v2)

> Second person, present tense, short lines, same voice as Part 1.
> `[DAY n AFTER]` = corner tag (days after the impact) · `[NOW · DAY n MISSING]` = present-day card (days since he vanished = days after impact + 10) · `(pause)` = held beat.
> New in v2:
> - His father: a paleontologist who died when he was seven. Through memories, not genes.
> - The locked room (how his mom found out).
> - "40 days after the impact = 50 days here" said clearly.
> - More real survival tricks (a grub log, the sand pit for water, keeping the fire).

## 0 · Seven Fish

[DAY 47 AFTER]
The last fish is gone.
You scrape something white off the wall of your tunnel and hold it up to the fire.
It's soft. It smells like the forest after rain.
Pip is watching you.
A week ago, you had seven fish.
(pause)
You have no food left. And nobody is coming.
At least, not for 66 million years.

## 1 · The Last of Them

[DAY 40 AFTER]
Forty days since the impact. Fifty since you woke up in this world.
Seven fish lie on the shelf you cut into the clay. One a day. You've done the math. Seven days.
Outside, the sky is the colour of wet ash. Some mornings, the ash is snow.

You remember the first weeks after the impact.
You weren't the only one who made it through the fire.
A few dinosaurs did too. You saw them.
A duck-bill, so thin you could count its ribs, chewing on burnt branches.
A young horned dinosaur, standing in the river. Just standing there.
But with no sun, nothing green grows. And with no plants, there's nothing to eat.
The plant-eaters starved first. Then the hunters that ate them.
The bigger you were, the more food you needed, and the faster you died.
It wasn't only the fire that killed the dinosaurs. It was the hunger after it.
By day forty, you hadn't seen one in weeks.
And the same hunger is now waiting at your door.

When you were small, your dad used to put you to bed under his dinosaur posters.
He dug up fossils for a living. He'd come home with dust in his hair and a story about a bone.
He died when you were seven. The posters stayed.
And every night, you lay under them and wished, just once, to see a real one.
(pause)
You got your wish.
You saw them all.
And you watched every one of them die.

## 2 · Meanwhile

[NOW · DAY 50 MISSING]
Sixty-six million years from now, your bedroom looks exactly the way you left it.
The next morning, your mom went in to wake you for school.
The bed was empty. Your door was locked from the inside. The window was shut.
The police still can't explain it.
There's a poster on every lamp post in town. Your face. MISSING.
Your mom hasn't moved a thing. The posters. The charger, still plugged into the wall.
On her nightstand, a photo: you, five years old, on your dad's shoulders, in front of a T. rex skeleton.

Your two best friends go back to the museum.
They stand in front of that same T. rex, where you filmed your video.
"I'd survive. Easy."
Nobody laughs this time.

## 3 · Eat What Eats the Dead

[DAY 47 AFTER]
Back in the tunnel, the last fish is gone.
One day with nothing. Then two.
Your hands start to shake. Pip gets thinner too.
The water still works. You dig the sand pit by the river again, like on day three. The grey water seeps in through the sand and comes out clearer.
But you can't eat water.

On day forty-nine, Pip does something strange.
He walks past the dead plants and goes straight for a rotten log.
He digs. And he comes out with a fat white grub.
Then another.
The forest is dead. But dead things are food, for something.
Beetles eat the rotting wood. Fungus eats the dead leaves. And the things that eat the dead are doing fine.
Scientists think that's what happened after the impact. The animals that lived on dead things kept going, in the soil and in the rivers, while everything that needed fresh plants starved.

So you dig next to Pip. Grubs. Beetles. And on the tunnel walls, that soft white fungus.
You don't know which fungus is safe. Pip does. He sniffs every kind. He eats the white. He won't touch the orange.
Neither will you.

Then you get smart.
You drag a wet, rotting log right next to your door, and pile dead leaves on top.
Every morning, there are new grubs under it.
For the first time since the impact, you have a farm.
Rule four: eat what eats the dead.
[LIST: FOOD: grub log, white fungus · WATER: sand pit · FIRE: dry wood stacked inside]

## 4 · A Message for Mom

[DAY 52 AFTER]
With food in your stomach, you can think again.
And the thought that comes back, every night, is your mom.
She doesn't know where you are. She never will.
You can't go back. You've known it for weeks. Tonight, you say it out loud.
"I can't go back."
Pip looks up at you.

Your dad used to say: "Every bone is a message from someone who was here."
(pause)
Maybe you can't go home. But maybe a message can.
You saw it happen to the T. rex at your door: buried fast in mud. That's how bones become fossils.
And you have a bone. The one you've been digging with since day four.
So you take a sharp stone, and you start to write on your shovel.
It takes three nights. Your fingers bleed. You go over every letter again and again, deep enough to last forever.
Two words.
The same two words you said in front of that skeleton, laughing.
I SURVIVED.

## 5 · The River at Night

Now it has to be buried. Fast. In mud. Where the river floods.
That means going down to the water, in the dark.
The river is still running, grey and cold, with ice along the edges in the mornings.
And the river has survivors of its own.
The crocodile. And something worse. A long, thin snout, like a crocodile's but narrower. A champsosaur. A fish hunter. It made it through too.
You wait until the moon is behind the smoke.
You crawl down the bank with the bone on your back.
You dig into the mud with your hands, right at the water's edge, where the river floods every spring.
Something moves in the water.
Pip squeaks. Once. Loud.
You throw yourself backwards, and the jaws snap shut where your hand just was.
(pause)
The bone is in the mud. You push the last handful over it and run.

Back in the tunnel, you sit with your back against the stone door, shaking.
It's done. Whatever happens to you now, something of you will last.

## 6 · Not Alone

[DAY 60 AFTER]
A few days later, Pip doesn't come back at night.
You wait. One night. Two.
On the third morning, he's at the entrance.
And he's not alone.
A second little face. Smaller. The same brown fur. The same black eyes.
She survived the dark too, in some other hole, under some other tree.
Pip isn't the last of his kind.
(pause)
You're happy for him. You really are.
But that night, the tunnel feels bigger than it ever has.

The cold is getting worse. The ice on the river doesn't melt by noon anymore.
Your tunnel was built for the fire. It wasn't built for winter.
You'll need somewhere deeper. Warmer.
But that's a problem for another day.
Tonight, you scratch a new line on the wall.
Friends: one.
You stop. And you add:
…and one more.

## 7 · Sixty-Six Million Years Later

[NOW]
North Dakota. A dig site, on a hot summer afternoon.
An old paleontologist brushes the dust off the shoulder blade of a duck-billed dinosaur.
Years ago, she dug next to your dad.
She stops.
There are scratches on the bone. Not teeth marks. Too straight. Too deep.
She calls the others over.
(pause)
If you could leave one message for 66 million years… what would you write on the bone?
Tell me in the comments.

Stay small. Stay alive.
