## 0 · The River Screams
[DAY 89 AFTER]
In the middle of the night, the river screams.
A long, low howl, right under you. Then a crack, like a gunshot, running away into the dark.
You're lying in a hole you dug in a snowdrift, with nothing between you and the river but your jacket.
Pip's heart is going fast against your neck.
The ice isn't breaking. (pause) You hope.
Yesterday, you had a tunnel, a fire and a door.
Today you have a backpack, a spear, and a shell full of fire.
And a river that runs east, all the way to the sea.
If the map is right.

## 1 · No Walls
[DAY 88 AFTER]
You leave in the morning, as soon as the grey light comes.
The ice is flat and grey, like a road no one else uses. The burnt forest slides past on both banks.
You count your steps. A thousand. Two thousand. Ten thousand. Then you stop counting.
Every hour, you open the fire shell and blow into it, gently. The fungus glows orange, then dark again.
As long as it glows, you have fire.

[GRAPHIC: seen from above, the frozen river winding east through the dead forest; a small red dot: YOU; a dashed line to a grey sea at the edge · THE SEA: ? DAYS]
On the book's map, the sea is one finger away.
You don't know how many days a finger is.

And out here, one thing matters more than how far you get.
In the tunnel, the night didn't matter. You had walls.
Out here, the night is what kills you. The cold drops, the wind rises, and you can't see the ice under your feet.
So before the light goes, you need a roof. Whatever you find. Wherever you are.
[RULE 6: BE UNDER A ROOF BEFORE DARK]

## 2 · The Ones Who Didn't Make It
Around midday, something dark lies across the ice ahead.
A duck-billed dinosaur, as long as a bus. It must have walked onto the river looking for water, back when the ice was thin. Now half of it is frozen into the river.
Your stomach says: meat. A whole mountain of it.
But it died long before it froze. Weeks of rot are locked inside that ice. And out here, one fever is the end.
Rule four says: eat what eats the dead. Not the dead.

Pip's mate pokes her head out of your pocket. She sniffs the air, jumps down, and digs into a fold of the frozen skin.
Beetles. Hundreds of them, frozen solid, packed into the folds.
She eats like she's never eaten before. Pip is right behind her.
Nobody had to teach them rule four.
You wait. And you fill one pocket with beetles, for later.

In the afternoon, the bank rises on your left, and from the snow on top, a skull looks down at you.
Three horns. A huge bony frill. A Triceratops, lying where it fell, the snow piled against its ribs.
You climb up and put your hand on the horn above its eye. It's taller than you.
"Every big animal you see will die." You've known that since the first day.
Knowing it is different from seeing it.
(pause)
You stay too long.
When you climb down, the grey is already turning dark.

The bank here is steep. Bare clay and stones. No drift, no hollow, nothing.
The wind picks up. You walk faster. Then you run, slipping, the fire shell knocking against your hip.
Then you see it: a shelf of rock sticking out of the bank. The wind blows the snow up over the top of it, and behind the rock, out of the wind, the snow falls and piles up, deep and soft.
You dig in from the side, right under the rock. The stone is your back wall. The snow is your roof.

It sounds crazy. A house made of snow.
But snow is mostly air, and trapped air is the best blanket there is.
Inside a snow cave, it can be twenty degrees warmer than outside. People in the Arctic have slept like this for thousands of years.

You push the spear through the roof, to make an air hole. Without it, the smoke from your fire would poison the air in there.
You keep the fire shell between your knees, away from the snow.
Pip and his mate crawl into your collar, their bellies full.
The last grey light goes out.
You made it. Just.

And for a while, it works.
Then the river starts to scream.

[CARD: THE ICE IS SINGING / it's getting thicker, not breaking]
The ice isn't breaking. It's getting thicker.
When the night gets colder, the ice shrinks and splits, and the cracks run for miles. It sounds like the world is breaking.
But ice that sings is ice that's getting stronger.
By morning, you're not afraid of the sound anymore. (pause)
Almost.

## 3 · Meanwhile
[NOW · DAY 98 MISSING]
Sixty-six million years later, it's the same night.
Your mom doesn't go home. She sits in the back room of the museum, across the table from the old woman, the bone between them.
"I know how this sounds," she says. "But you're the only person who has seen what I've seen."
And then she tells her about the dream.
Grey snow falling. Burnt trees. A small fire under the roots of a huge tree, at the edge of a river. And a door. One flat stone.
The old woman doesn't laugh.
She is quiet for a long time.
She never told your mom exactly where the bone came from. Not just the hills. The clay of an old river channel.
"Animals dig homes in riverbanks," she says at last. "When the river floods, the holes fill with mud. Sometimes, millions of years later, we find them. Turned to stone."
She doesn't say anything else. She doesn't have to.

[NOW · DAY 99 MISSING]
The next morning, your mom calls the two people who knew you best, after her.
Your two best friends are at her door within the hour. One of them is holding a phone.
The class trip. A few months before you disappeared.
You, laughing at the camera, a giant skeleton behind you.
"If they ever sent me back to the dinosaurs? I'd survive. Easy."
Your mom watches it four times.
The old woman watches it once. Then she stops it, and zooms in. Not on you. On the skeleton behind you.
A long scar, healed into the bone of the snout.
"I know this animal," she says. "I dug it out of the ground thirty years ago."
"Where?"
"Two kilometres from where I found the bone."
(pause)
That evening, four people get into one car. A mother. An old woman with a shovel in the trunk. And your two best friends, who won't take no for an answer.
They drive toward the badlands, where it all came out of the ground.
