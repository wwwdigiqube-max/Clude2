## 0 · The Shape Under the Ice

[DAY 70 AFTER]
You're lying flat on the ice, in the dark of the morning.
Under your face, through a hole the size of a hand, something moves.
Slow. Grey. As long as your forearm.
Your spear is above it. The stone tip shakes a little in your hand.
You've eaten grubs for three weeks.
This would be the first animal you have ever tried to kill.
(pause)
You strike.
And you miss.

## 1 · The Cold Comes After

[DAY 66 AFTER]
Four days earlier.
The fire came first. On the day of the impact, the sky burned and the forests caught fire.
Then the smoke and the dust went up, high, and stayed there.
The sun is still up there somewhere. It just can't get through.
Scientists call it an impact winter. In some places, it may have gotten colder than any winter the dinosaurs had ever seen.
That's where you are now.

The river is lower than it's ever been. The snow doesn't melt, so nothing runs into it.
Ice grows from both banks, a little more every night.
But the fish didn't disappear. They were always there.
In winter, fish crowd into the deepest pools of a river and barely move, to save energy.
Your hands could never reach them.
Now you have a spear.

## 2 · The Book

You haven't opened the book since the night the fire died.
It's been at the back of the nest chamber, wrapped in your empty backpack, the last dry thing you own.
Tonight, you take it out.
The cover still shows the T. rex skeleton. Your dad's T. rex.
You turn the pages by the fire.
A tiny brown mammal among ferns. Pip's great-great-grandfather, maybe.
A burrow under tree roots.
And on the last painted page: a turtle and a frog, at the edge of a freshwater river.
(pause)
Under the picture, a line you never read on night one.
In winter, they bury themselves in the mud at the bottom, and sleep.
(pause)
The book is right. Turtles and frogs made it through the end of the world. So did fish. So did the crocodile.
Things that live in fresh water did better than almost anything on land.
The river isn't just water. It's the last place with meat.

You need tinder for tomorrow's fire. The book would burn well.
You tear out the two blank pages at the back. Only those.
The rest of it is the only teacher you have.

## 3 · Meanwhile

[NOW · DAY 80 MISSING]
Sixty-six million years later, in a museum lab, the old woman has not gone home.
The bone from North Dakota is on her table, under a lamp.
The lab dated the rock around it: sixty-six million years old. The layer was never disturbed.
Her colleagues say it has to be a prank. Someone carved it, and someone buried it.
She knows how to read rock. Nobody buried this.
She takes one photo of the marks, close up. Then she covers the bone with a cloth, and turns off the lamp.

## 4 · Learn to See

[DAY 70 AFTER]
Back on the ice. You missed.
Not because you were slow. Because the water lied to you.
Water bends light. A fish under water is never where it looks. It's a little deeper, and a little closer.
Fishermen have known this for thousands of years. You learn it in one morning.
You wait. Your fingers go numb. Pip sits by the hole, still as a stone.
The grey shape drifts back.
This time, you aim below it.
(pause)
The spear jerks in your hands.

You carry it home inside your jacket, like something precious.
You cook it on a flat stone, the way you dried fish in the summer.
It's the best thing you have ever eaten.
Pip and his mate get the head. They don't share.

The next day, you go back to the bank where the book's turtle lived.
You dig into the soft mud under the ice, slowly, the way you buried the bone.
Your hand finds something hard and round.
A turtle, asleep, cold as a stone.
You don't enjoy what comes next. But you don't waste any of it, either.
Even the shell. It holds water. It holds coals.
For the first time since the impact, you have meat.
And the second question mark comes off the wall.
Rule five: learn from the ones who made it.

## 5 · Go Deeper

[DAY 78 AFTER]
Then the real cold comes.
One morning, the water in the turtle shell is frozen solid next to the fire.
The tunnel walls sparkle with frost.
Your tunnel is only an arm's length under the ground. That's not enough.
A little deeper, the ground hardly changes temperature, winter or summer. Every burrowing animal knows it.
Pip knew it on day one.
So you dig down. With the sharp stone and your hands, in the back of the nest chamber.
It takes four days. When you're done, there's a hollow under the floor, just big enough to curl up in.
The air down there is cold. But it's not freezing.
Pip and his mate move in before you do. They drag in dry grass, and a piece of your sleeve.
You let them.
The first question mark comes off the wall too.

## 6 · How Long?

Now you have fire. Water. Meat. A shelter.
And one night, lying in the hollow, you ask yourself the question you've been avoiding.
How long?
How long can you live like this? Day after day, in a hole, waiting for a sun that might not come back for years?
You open the book again, to the first page.
A map. North America, sixty-six million years ago.
Down the middle, there's a sea. A long, shallow inland sea, east of where you are.
And you remember something your dad used to say at the dinner table, when the winters were long.
"The ocean is the world's blanket. It holds the heat."
(pause)
Near the sea, the dark would be warmer. There would be fish. There would be things that live on the shore.
The river runs east.
Every river runs to the sea.
(pause)
You don't sleep that night.
You write a new list on the wall, in charcoal.
Not to survive another week. To leave.

## 7 · The Dream

[NOW · DAY 88 MISSING]
That same night, sixty-six million years later, your mom has a dream.
She's standing in a forest that's grey and burnt, under a sky the colour of wet ash.
Snow is falling, but it's not white. It's grey.
And by a small fire, under the roots of a huge black tree, there's a boy in a beanie.
He's thinner. His sleeves are torn.
He doesn't look up. He talks to the fire, the way he used to talk to her from his room.
"Mom. I'm alive. I wrote you something. I don't know if it'll get to you."
She calls out. "Where are you?"
He looks up.
(pause)
She wakes up with her heart pounding, and it's still dark.
She lies there for a long time, replaying it. The snow. The fire. The beanie.
That was his dad's beanie.

At the same moment, in the hollow under the tunnel floor, you're awake too.
You do this every night. You talk to her out loud, so you don't forget her voice.
"Mom. I'm alive. I wrote you something."
It's just a thing you say.

## 8 · The Handwriting

The next morning, your mom is at the museum before it opens.
She doesn't really know why. She just goes where you always went.
The old woman is there, in a side room, with a cloth over something on the table.
Your mom tells her about the dream. She knows how it sounds. She tells her anyway.
"He said he wrote me something."
The old woman doesn't laugh.
She's quiet for a long time. Then she takes out her phone, and shows your mom one photo.
A bone. Sixty-six million years old. And on it, carved deep, three words.
Your mom doesn't read them at first. She looks at the letters.
The M that starts with a long first stroke. The little dash instead of a dot. The way the letters lean, like he's in a hurry.
(pause)
"That's his handwriting."

[DAY 90 AFTER]
And sixty-six million years earlier, on a cold morning, you tie the turtle shell to your belt with a strip of t-shirt, slide the book inside your jacket, and pick up your spear.
Pip is in your pocket. His mate is in the other one.
You look back at the stone door one last time.
Then you walk to the river, and you follow it east.
(pause)
If you had to leave your shelter and walk into the dark, what's the one thing you would take?

Stay small. Stay alive.
